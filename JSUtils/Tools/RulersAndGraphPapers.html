~*~ RULERS AND GRAPH PAPERS ~*~

Three small tools based on my C++ versions:
a linear ruler, graph paper, and a circular ruler.

These rulers are made to substitute a normal physical ruler. I made this program because four things:
1) My physical ruler is losing the ink of certain marks, and it also got a new crack in it.
2) Using another ruler, i noticed that some metric lines are not perfectly aligned from one ruler to another. These rulers are made for school and not high level precision (not that i truly need it), so a computer may in fact be more accurate than it.
After i made such programs, i noticed that after certain steps, even tough the marks are on a consistent place, the alignment aren't, one of my rulers have marks where the ink is not centered to the correct place, even if this misalignment is placed on the correct metric.
The circular ruler, i noticed is useful since i don't have a ruler for measure of the size of something based on circumference. I also noticed it is easier to measure angles if i draw on a paper over my screen.
3) The screen light helps when drawing on a paper over the screen. Also, the image frame is a good indicator of alignment.
4) A digital ruler is easier to carry everywhere or to access it while not needing to worry if you are carrying it or not, the problem is just the calibration. It also does not break and get lost.

* Find the correct scale for the unit size you want, it depends on your screen size and resolution.
* * For example, 'Scale = 36.414' is '1 cm' in my screen (seems different in HTML Canvas than C++, which is '36.44813248', it may not be correctly aligned for big measures. Also, Canvas stroke is two pixels wide). This can be read as 'pixels per cm', but you can use other measures by modifying the 'Scale', ex.:
'36.414 * 2.54' would turn the rulers plotted in my screen as based on inches (pixels per inches).

* If you calibrate based on the page view, then it might differ from when you right-click and open the image in a new tab to either use it or download it.
* * In my screen, 'Scale = 33.4' is '1 cm' if not opening the image in a new tab.

####### CIRCULAR RULER #######



Circle ruler is just like a normal ruler, but as if you bend it until it becomes a circle.

* Let's say you want 6 lines and 6 smaller lines, you can then double the scale and use 'Measure_size = 6' and 'SubDiv = 2', pick 'Circumference' option, and this is going to make a circle with 12cm circumference with marks at a clock position.
* If you use the 'Circumference' option, the circle will be divided by the 'Measure_size' value. That means that if you connect each metric line, you can form a regular polygon.
* If you want to make angle measures, change to a new scale, let's say your scale is 30, and you want to measure degrees with metrics at every 15 degrees (360/15=24) with a ruler of 50cm. You do this: 'Scale = (cm/metric_lines)*Scale_Actual = (50/24)*30'. Then 'SubDiv = 15'.
* The metric lines are OUTSIDE the ruler circumference, that means, size measure is from the inner circle.
